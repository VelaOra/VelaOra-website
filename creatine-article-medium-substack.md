## What Creatine Actually Is

Creatine is a naturally occurring compound your body makes from three amino acids — glycine, arginine, and methionine. You find it primarily in muscle and brain tissue. You make some yourself, and you get small amounts from red meat and fish. But most women — particularly those eating less meat as they age — are running significantly below optimal levels.

Women naturally have lower creatine stores than men, at around 70–80% of male levels. This means we're more often deficient, and we often have more to gain from supplementation than men do. The research is only now catching up to this fact.

Here's the mechanism: creatine helps regenerate ATP — adenosine triphosphate — your body's primary energy currency. When you need a burst of energy (lifting something heavy, thinking hard under pressure, getting through 3pm on four hours of sleep), your body burns through ATP faster than it can make it from scratch. Creatine bridges that gap almost instantly.

One elegant function. And from it flows an extraordinary range of benefits — because almost every cell in your body runs on ATP.

## Why Perimenopause Changes Everything

Here is what nobody prepares you for: the years before your final period are when your body starts losing the biological scaffolding it has relied on for decades. Oestrogen — not just a reproductive hormone but a full-body protector — begins its decline. And that decline touches nearly everything creatine happens to support.

**Muscle.** Oestrogen plays a direct role in muscle protein synthesis. As it declines, you lose muscle mass at an accelerating rate — up to 1% per year after 40, faster still after the final period. Muscle loss isn't just aesthetic. It affects your metabolism, bone density, balance, fall risk, and long-term independence. Creatine combined with resistance training is the most evidence-backed intervention available for preserving and building muscle mass in midlife women.

**Brain and cognitive function.** Creatine crosses into brain tissue. Higher brain creatine levels are associated with better memory, faster processing speed, sharper attention, and reduced mental fatigue. A 2022 review in *Nutrients* found creatine significantly improved cognitive performance in females — with effects that were *larger* than those seen in males.

**Mood and emotional stability.** A brain with adequate energy regulates mood more effectively. The CONCRET-MENOPA trial (2025, Journal of the American Nutrition Association) — the first randomised controlled trial specifically targeting creatine in perimenopausal and postmenopausal women — found meaningful improvements in mood swing severity and emotional balance after just eight weeks. No adverse effects were identified.

**Bone density.** Oestrogen protects bone. Its decline accelerates bone loss, particularly in the years following the final period. Research suggests creatine combined with resistance training significantly reduces bone resorption markers compared to exercise alone.

**Sleep recovery.** Up to 47% of perimenopausal women experience significant sleep disruption. Research shows that a single higher dose of creatine can meaningfully restore cognitive function after poor sleep — more on that below.

### What the Trials Actually Show

The CONCRET-MENOPA trial (2025) was the first RCT specifically in perimenopausal and postmenopausal women. Thirty-six participants, eight weeks. Even low doses produced measurable improvements in brain energy, cognitive function, reaction time, mood swing severity, and emotional balance. No adverse effects. This single trial has accelerated a significant shift in clinical thinking.

A 2022 review in *Nutrients* found creatine significantly improved cognitive performance in females — particularly verbal memory and processing speed — with effects larger than those seen in males.

A 2021 meta-analysis in *European Journal of Sport Science* found creatine supplementation significantly improved upper and lower body strength in women, with greatest benefits combined with resistance training.

Candow et al. (2021, *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise*) found creatine plus resistance training significantly reduced bone resorption markers versus exercise alone in older adults.

*References: CONCRET-MENOPA (J Am Nutr Assoc, 2025); Smith-Ryan et al. 2025 lifespan review; Xu et al. (Front Nutr, 2024); Candow et al. (MSSE, 2021); Lanhers et al. (EJSS, 2021).*

## But Won't It Make Me Bulky?

No. And I want to address this head-on because it is the single biggest reason women avoid creatine — and it has cost us years of benefit.

Creatine does not make women bulky. What it does is help you hold onto and build *lean muscle* — which, after 40, becomes increasingly difficult without active support. Lean muscle makes you look stronger and more defined, not bigger. The physiques associated with anabolic supplements are the result of years of high-volume training, specific nutrition protocols, and compounds in an entirely different category. Creatine is not in that conversation.

Dr Stacy Sims is direct on this: women who avoid creatine because of bulk fears are leaving one of their most powerful health tools on the table.

In the first week or two, you may notice a small increase on the scale — typically 0.5–1.5kg. This is water being drawn *into* your muscle cells, not under your skin. It's a sign the creatine is working. It levels off quickly, and once the energy and strength benefits kick in, the number on the scale becomes irrelevant.

## How to Take It — Dose, Timing, and What I Actually Do

**My daily dose: 5g.** I stir it into a glass of water every morning. It dissolves almost completely, has essentially no taste, and takes about ten seconds. No ceremony, no protocol. Just 5g every day, consistently.

**The evidence-based range** is 3–5g daily. Dr Stacy Sims recommends 3–5g for ongoing muscle, brain, and bone support. You do not need to do a loading phase — consistent daily use saturates your muscle stores within 3–4 weeks. Cycling on and off is not necessary and not supported by evidence.

**Timing** matters less than consistency. Some evidence suggests slightly better uptake when taken with carbohydrates and protein (insulin helps drive creatine into cells), so a morning meal or post-workout shake works well. But if you take it in your coffee every day, that works too.

**Which form:** Creatine monohydrate. Not creatine HCL, not buffered creatine, not creatine ethyl ester. Monohydrate has the most research, the best safety record, and is significantly cheaper. Look for **Creapure® certification** — a German manufacturing standard for pharmaceutical-grade purity. "Micronised" simply means smaller particles that dissolve more easily — convenient, no physiological difference.

## The Sleep Rescue Dose — What Stacy Sims Does After a Bad Night

This is the part of the creatine story most people don't know about yet.

Sleep deprivation depletes brain energy stores. When you don't sleep well, your brain's creatine reserves drop — which is one of the reasons a bad night leaves you foggy, reactive, and emotionally flat. Research shows that a single higher dose of creatine — approximately **0.35g per kilogram of bodyweight** — can significantly restore cognitive function after poor sleep. For most women this is around 20g, taken as a single morning dose. You're flooding your brain with the energy substrate it burned through overnight.

Dr Stacy Sims uses this protocol herself on mornings after genuinely poor sleep.

**How the dosing works:**

- **Daily dose:** 3–5g — your ongoing foundation, every day
- **Sleep rescue dose:** ~0.35g per kg bodyweight as a single morning dose — roughly 20–25g for most women. Occasional use only, not daily.

*Example: 65kg = ~22g. 70kg = ~24.5g. Many women round to 20g as a practical starting point. Dissolve in a large glass of water or smoothie.*

*Reference: Xu et al. (Front Nutr, 2024); McMorris et al. (Neuropsychologia, 2006).*

## Does It Work Without Exercise?

Yes — but considerably better with resistance training. The evidence for muscle, strength, and bone is most powerful when creatine and exercise are paired. But creatine delivers meaningful benefits for brain function, mood, and cellular energy regardless of exercise status.

If you're not currently lifting weights, creatine is a good reason to consider it — even two sessions per week produces measurable benefits. Don't wait until you're exercising to start the creatine. Start both.

## Is It Safe? The Kidney Question

Creatine is one of the most extensively safety-tested supplements in history. Over 1,000 studies. Decades of clinical trials. The answer is yes — creatine monohydrate is safe for healthy adults at these doses.

The kidney concern is a misunderstanding. Creatine metabolism produces creatinine, a waste product filtered by the kidneys. If you supplement creatine and then have blood tests, your creatinine levels may look elevated — which can appear to be a kidney function problem to a GP who doesn't know you're supplementing. It isn't. Just mention it to your doctor so the result is interpreted correctly.

If you have existing kidney disease, speak to your specialist first. For everyone else, decades of evidence are clear: no risk to kidney function at 3–5g daily.

## Can You Take It With HRT?

Yes. Creatine and HRT work through entirely different mechanisms. No interactions have been identified. If anything, they complement each other — HRT addressing the hormonal root, creatine supporting the muscle, brain, and energy systems that benefit from additional cellular fuel.

## Questions I Get Asked

**How long before I notice a difference?** Most women notice improved energy in training within 2–3 weeks. Cognitive benefits tend to emerge around the 4–6 week mark. Give the daily dose three months before judging it — and this time, understand what it's doing so you know what to look for.

**What about creatine gummies?** Skip them. Creatine degrades in acidic or liquid environments, so most gummies deliver far less than labelled. The dose per gummy is also tiny — you'd need 8–12 to hit 5g, at significant cost. They exist because monohydrate powder has a branding problem, not because gummies work better.

**It gave me bloating before.** More common with cheap or non-micronised creatine. Switch to micronised monohydrate, take it with food, and try 3g rather than 5g if it persists.

**Can I take the 20g dose every day?** No — it's an occasional rescue strategy, not a daily practice. Your 5g daily is the foundation.

**I don't exercise. Is there still a point?** Yes, particularly for brain energy and mood. But please also consider two resistance sessions a week — the combination is where the real magic is.

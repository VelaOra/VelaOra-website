Separation & Divorce

Navigating the end of a chapter

Practical guidance, plain-English legal navigation and real human support, for one of the hardest things women go through.

The gap between crisis and lawyers

In New Zealand, the gap between Women's Refuge (crisis support) and a $400-an-hour family lawyer is wide, and women going through separation often fall into it. They're left without clear information about their rights, the legal process, or how to protect themselves and their children.

VelaOra sits in that gap. This page is navigation, not legal advice: plain-English explanations of the NZ process and where to find support, so you can walk into conversations with a lawyer or the court better prepared.

Protection orders in New Zealand

A protection order is a civil court order that stops someone being violent, threatening or intimidating towards you or your children. You apply through the Family Court, and you can apply without notice, which means the other person isn't told before a judge looks at it. Legal aid is available if you qualify, and Women's Refuge can support you through the process.

Recognising family violence

Family violence takes many forms, and not all of them are visible. Being able to name what's happening is often the first step.

  • Physical violence, intimidation and threats
  • Emotional and psychological abuse, including coercive control
  • Financial control: restricting money, work or access to accounts
  • Stalking and digital abuse, such as tracking your phone or monitoring messages
  • Isolating you from friends, family and support
  • Reproductive coercion

These are all recognised under New Zealand's Family Violence Act 2018. You don't need to have experienced physical violence for it to be family violence, and there's no severity threshold before you can ask for help or take legal steps.

Advocating for yourself through a legal system is exhausting when you're already struggling. You don't have to navigate it alone, and you don't have to understand it all at once.

If you have children

Most parents are expected to try to agree on care arrangements outside court first. The Ministry of Justice runs a free Parenting Through Separation course, and recommends doing it before Family Dispute Resolution (mediation). FDR may be free for you. If there's family violence, you may not have to go through mediation, so tell the provider or your lawyer.

  • Parenting Through Separation (justice.govt.nz)
  • Family Court and family justice information
  • Legal aid and your local Community Law Centre for free legal help
  • Citizens Advice Bureau for free, confidential information

Looking after yourself through it

When life is in crisis, the basics are the first thing to go. A few things genuinely help your body cope:

  • Regular meals, with protein at each. Unstable blood sugar amplifies anxiety and emotional reactivity.
  • Protect your sleep. It's usually the first casualty, and everything feels harder without it. Our sleep article has practical ideas.
  • Go gently with alcohol. It's a depressant that worsens anxiety, disrupts sleep and clouds decisions. Even a temporary break makes a difference.
  • Lean on people. Tell one person you trust what's going on.
